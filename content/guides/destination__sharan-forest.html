Sharan Forest guide: jeep track, stay, walks and best season

Quick answer: Sharan Forest is a dense pine, fir, spruce and deodar forest above the village of Paras in the Kaghan Valley, Mansehra District, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. A steep jeep track climbs from Paras to the Sharan rest area at about 2,500 m. Go between May and September, travel up by 4×4 jeep with a local driver, and stay overnight in the forest rest house, huts or a camp if you want the full experience. It is quieter than Siri Paye and easy to combine with Shogran or Malkandi.

Sharan is what many people imagine when they think of northern Pakistan's forests: tall, straight conifers, moss-covered slopes, clear streams and cool shade even in July. Unlike Shogran, which has a bazaar and several hotels, Sharan has only a handful of simple places to stay, so it feels remote while still being reachable in a day from Shogran.

Where is Sharan Forest?

Sharan sits on the forested hillside above Paras, a village on the main Kaghan road between Kiwai and Kaghan town. To get there you follow the Kunhar River up the valley from Balakot, pass the Kiwai turn for Shogran and Kawai Waterfall, continue to Paras, then turn off onto the jeep track that climbs through the forest to Sharan.

How to get to Sharan Forest

From Islamabad

Drive about 255 km via the Hazara Motorway, Mansehra, Balakot and Kiwai to Paras, which takes roughly 5½-6½ hours in normal traffic. At Paras, switch to a local 4×4 jeep for the climb. Your car can wait in Paras or at your hotel.

From Shogran

Drive down the 7-9 km road from Shogran to Kiwai, continue up the valley along the Kaghan road to Paras, then take the jeep up to Sharan. Many visitors do it as a day trip from Shogran, leaving after breakfast and returning before dark.

Why a jeep is needed

The Sharan track is steep, narrow and unpaved, with sharp bends and loose surfaces that turn slippery after rain. Local drivers use it daily and know how the conditions change. Do not try it in an ordinary car. Hotels in Paras and Malkandi, such as Parbat View Hotel, can help arrange the jeep.

Things to do in Sharan

  • Forest walks. Easy trails lead from the rest area into the trees and along small streams. Even a short walk feels far from the road.
  • Picnics. Grassy clearings make good picnic spots. Bring food from Paras, as options at the top are limited.
  • Overnight stay. Spending a night lets you see the forest in the quiet of early morning and at dusk.
  • Photography. Light filtering through tall conifers, mist after rain and green clearings make Sharan a favourite with photographers.
  • Combine with Malkandi. Malkandi Forest and its riverside hotels are nearby, making a relaxed two-day forest trip.

With two or three nights, the best plan is both: Siri Paye early one morning from Shogran, and Sharan the next day from Paras or Malkandi.

Practical tips

  • Book overnight stays in Sharan well ahead for June, July and August.
  • Carry warm clothes; the forest is cold at night even in summer.
  • Bring enough cash; there is no ATM in Shogran or Sharan, and the last reliable ATM is in Balakot.
  • Mobile signal is weak in parts of the forest, so tell your hotel or our team your plans.
  • Keep the forest clean and do not light fires outside designated areas.

How long does the jeep take from Paras to Sharan?

The track is steep and rough, so the jeep moves slowly. Plan for roughly an hour each way including photo stops, and confirm the timing with your driver because it changes with weather and track conditions.

Is Sharan Forest open in winter?

Snow makes the track difficult or impassable in winter. Sharan is best visited from May to September, and we confirm track conditions before every booking.

Is Sharan Forest good for families?

Yes. The jeep ride is bumpy but short, the walks are gentle and the forest is calm. Families with young children usually visit as a day trip and stay in Shogran or Malkandi.

Is Sharan Forest the same as Malkandi Forest?

No, but they are close neighbours in the same part of the Kaghan Valley, both reached via Paras. Many visitors combine them in one trip.
